security

Your key never leaves the machine.

wake signs a bounded intent locally. The guard network can submit that intent when your condition holds — and nothing wider.

Three steps

001

Sign locally

The key stays on your machine. What leaves is one intent, bounded by a condition and an expiry.

002

Watch the chain

Guards poll for your condition. They see the intent and the trigger, never the key.

003

Fire once

When the condition holds, a guard submits. Anything outside what you signed, the chain rejects.

What one intent looks like

{
  "intent": "close",
  "filter": "positions | grep MNGO",
  "condition": { "field": "price", "below": 0.92 },
  "expires": "72h",
  "signed": "locally"
}

What dog will not do

It does not store keys.

It does not act on its own.

Nothing happens except what you put in wake or nap.

It does not give advice.

No sorting by yield, no defaults. An order is advice and a default is advice, so there are none.

It does not add commands that will not pipe.

That is the only defence against turning into a dashboard with a prompt attached.
