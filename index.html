● Thesis

Everything built on Solana assumes state is complex and must be displayed: charts, cards, dashboards.

Unix answered it differently. Reduce everything to one kind of object, give it a grammar, and let people assemble what they need. dog takes that bet further: your chain is a filesystem — and every file deserves a guard.

READ

dog, ls, grep, sort, diff, scan

One object type, read one way. No defaults, no rankings.

ACT

sweep, hiss, nine

Clean up dust, surface risk, roll back. Signing never leaves your machine.

GUARD

nap, wake, watch, purr

A condition is an object too. Close the laptop; the dog stays awake.

COMPOSE

paw, fetch

Save a pipe as a name. Fetch somebody else's. Pipes cover what buttons cannot.

● Questions

Where do my keys go?
Nowhere. Signing stays on your machine and dog never holds a key. What leaves is a single intent, bounded by a condition and an expiry. A guard can submit that intent and nothing else.
What if a guard never runs my task?
Guards post collateral and lose part of it for a task they took and missed. A task nobody executed is compensated from the shared pool. Not "we will do our best" — it costs us more not to.
Will it tell me what to buy?
No. dog shows you what is, and does what you say. Nothing is sorted by yield, nothing is marked worth a look. You get statements of fact and you decide.
